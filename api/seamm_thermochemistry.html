seamm_thermochemistry.atct module#

A local copy of the Active Thermochemical Tables (ATcT) and the rules for choosing an entry.

ATcT (https://atct.anl.gov) publishes enthalpies of formation at 0 K and 298.15 K for a few thousand species, derived from one thermochemical network (TN), so the values are mutually consistent and correlated. This module keeps a complete copy of one version of the tables on disk, builds an index from canonical SMILES to the ATcT entries, chooses the right entry for a molecule, and propagates the network covariance through a reaction.

Layout of the cache, one directory per TN version:

<base>/ATcT/<version>/
    atct_all.json       the /api/v1/all/ download, unmodified
    metadata.json       version, source URL, download date, SHA-256
    index.json          canonical SMILES -> ATcT IDs, built locally
    covariance.json     covariance blocks fetched so far, by version

where <base> is the installation’s Parameters/thermochemistry directory by default. Nothing here is distributed with SEAMM: each installation downloads its own copy, and the copy is never modified, so results can be reproduced.

Conventions that matter (Ruscic 2004, 2005; Ruscic & Bross 2025):

  • The listed uncertainties, and the covariance matrix, are 95 % confidence limits, valid at 298.15 K only. There is no 0 K uncertainty.

  • A plain (g) entry is the species in its ground state: at 0 K the lowest level of the lowest conformer; at 298.15 K a thermal average over conformers. Conformers, stereoisomers, spin states and nuclear-spin isomers are separate entries, labelled in the formula’s state, e.g. (g, trans), (g, triplet), (g, para).

  • Ions use the stationary-electron convention.

  • Never mix TN versions in one reaction.

RDKit is needed only to build the index and to canonicalize query SMILES; it is imported lazily, so the rest of the package does not depend on it.

seamm_thermochemistry.cbh module#

Connectivity-based hierarchy (CBH) reactions for a molecule.

The CBH of Ramabhadran and Raghavachari (J. Chem. Theory Comput. 7, 2094 (2011); Raghavachari et al., J. Chem. Theory Comput. 19, 3763 (2023)) builds, from the hydrogen-suppressed molecular graph alone, one error-cancelling reaction per rung:

Rung

Products (capped with H to the parent’s valence)

Reactants

CBH-0

the hydride of every heavy atom

H2, to balance H

CBH-1

every heavy-atom bond

N0(atom) x (deg-1)

CBH-2

every heavy atom with its heavy neighbours

N0(bond) per bond

CBH-3

every bond with the neighbours of both atoms

N1(atom) x (deg-1)

CBH-4

every atom with two shells of neighbours

N1(bond) per bond

Here N_k(atom) is the atom with all heavy atoms within k bonds, N_k(bond) is the bond’s two atoms with all heavy atoms within k bonds of either, and deg is the number of heavy neighbours. Odd rungs (2k+1) have N_k(bond) products and N_k(atom) reactants with coefficient (deg - 1); even rungs (2k >= 2) have N_k(atom) products and N_{k-1}(bond) reactants with coefficient 1. Identical fragments are merged and species on both sides cancel. The target itself has coefficient -1. This reproduces every published reaction we checked (see tests/test_cbh.py).

Every fragment is the induced subgraph: it keeps the bonds, bond orders, formal charges and unpaired electrons its atoms have in the parent, and every cut bond is replaced by hydrogens that restore the atom’s valence in the parent. Double-bond stereochemistry and the relative configuration of stereocentres are kept, so cis and trans fragments are different species.

Groups that should not be broken (aromatic ring systems, three- and four-membered rings, charge-separated or hypervalent groups such as nitro and sulfonyl, and whole inorganic ions) are contracted into single nodes of the graph first (“coarse-grained” or “ring-preserved” CBH). The hierarchy on the contracted graph is shallower: for 4-chlorotoluene with the ring protected, CBH-1 is Cl-C6H4-CH3 + C6H6 -> C6H5Cl + C6H5CH3 and CBH-2 is already trivial.

Limitations: the simple overlap formula is valid only when every unprotected ring of size r satisfies rung <= r - 2; such a rung is reported as unavailable. Aromatic rings that are not protected are Kekulized, which makes CBH-3 and higher depend on the Kekule structure chosen. A delocalized charge or spin must be localized on one atom by the input SMILES.

seamm_thermochemistry.db module#

SQLite-backed store for atomic reference energies.

Every SEAMM code that reports a formation-referenced energy (Gaussian, Psi4, ORCA, VASP, …) needs the same two pieces of data per element: an experimental reference (heat of formation, entropy, standard-state description) and one or more computed atomic reference energies (one per code/method/settings combination). Today each plugin carries its own copy of both as a wide, mostly-empty CSV. This module replaces that with a single, shared, relational store – one element row per element, one atom_energy row per (element, code, method, ref_type, settings) combination – with room for the provenance a shared reference dataset needs (who computed it, when, with what).

Two reference conventions are both first-class (ref_type):

  • "atom": energy of the isolated, gas-phase atom. This is the Gaussian/Psi4/ORCA convention, and the one to use for a physically anchored, cross-code-comparable energy of formation.

  • "element_phase": energy per atom of the element’s standard-state phase (bulk metal, graphite, O2(g), …). This is VASP’s existing convention (element_energies.csv) – no experimental anchor needed, but the result is referenced to the computed elemental phases, not real formation energies. It also serves as a fallback for elements (e.g. Mn) where the free atom is a poor DFT reference.

See formation.py for the arithmetic that consumes this store.

seamm_thermochemistry.derive module#

Derive DfH(X, 0K) from the 298 K experimental data already in the store.

The master reference sheet only has ΔfH°(0K) filled in for H and He; every other element has ΔfH°(298K) plus the two H(298K)-H(0K) corrections needed to convert it, via the standard thermochemical identity:

DfH(X, 0K) = DfH(X, 298K) - [H298-H0]_atom + [H298-H0]_standard_state

h298_minus_h0_std_state is tabulated per formula unit of the standard state, not per atom – for a diatomic standard state (H2, N2, O2, F2, Cl2, Br2) it must be divided by 2 to get a per-atom contribution; for the monatomic/solid standard states used by every other element in range, no division is needed. Verified against the two elements the master sheet already gives directly: H (218 - 6.197 + 8.468/2 = 216.03, matches 216.034) and He (0 - 6.197 + 6.197 = 0, matches 0), both to within the sheet’s own rounding.

seamm_thermochemistry.formation module#

The shared arithmetic: atomization and formation energies from a ThermoDB.

Every code (Gaussian, Psi4, ORCA, VASP, …) that reports a formation energy is doing the same subtraction:

atomization = sum(n_X * E_ref(X)) - E(system)

and, when an experimental anchor is available,

formation = sum(n_X * DfH(X)) - atomization

They differ only in the choice of E_ref (an isolated gas atom, or an atom of the element’s standard-state phase – ref_type on ThermoDB) and whether an experimental anchor is added at all. See ~/Sites/reference-energy/2026-07-24_reference-energy/ for the full design rationale, including why the arbitrary code-dependent zero of a raw total energy is the problem this solves, and the “no thermochemistry -> energy of formation, not enthalpy” case this module is aimed at.

Sign convention, checked against the existing gaussian_step / psi4_step / vasp_step implementations:

  • atomization_energy matches Gaussian/Psi4’s “E atomization” (>0 for a bound system: separated atoms sit higher in energy than the molecule).

  • formation_energy(..., anchor=True) matches the existing DfH_at - H_atomization construction (Gaussian/Psi4’s enthalpy of formation), generalized to also give a ZPE-free “energy of formation” when the 0 K anchor is used and system_energy excludes ZPE.

  • formation_energy(..., anchor=False) matches VASP’s existing DfE0 = E(system) - sum(n_X * mu_X) (materials formation-energy convention, no experimental data required) – note this is -atomization_energy, not atomization_energy itself; the two conventions report the quantity with opposite sign, which is why they are two functions rather than one flag.

DfH(T): enthalpy of formation from the elements, at temperature T.

Generalizes formation_energy(..., anchor=True, anchor_at_0K=True) from the electronic-only, 0 K quantity to a real temperature. The Hess cycle is elements (standard states, T) -> gas atoms (T) -> molecule (T). The gas atoms’ own H(T)-H(0) appears in both halves of the cycle and cancels exactly, leaving the standard-state elements’ thermal enthalpy (the form used by G4 and similar composite methods):

DfH(T) = sum(n_X * [DfH0(X, 0 K) - (H_std(X, T) - H_std(X, 0))])
         - [sum(n_X * E_ref(X)) - H_system]

where H_std is the enthalpy of the element’s standard state, per atom (ThermoDB.element.h298_minus_h0_std_state, halved for the diatomic gases). Equivalently, DfH(T) = sum(n_X * DfH0(X, T)) - [atomization enthalpy at T]; at 298.15 K the two forms use the same tabulated data and agree to rounding.

Approximation: the standard-state H(T)-H(0) is tabulated at 298.15 K only and is scaled linearly in T elsewhere – see _std_state_thermal_enthalpy for the size of the error. At 298.15 K the result is exact.

DfG(T): Gibbs energy of formation from the elements, at T.

Same Hess-cycle construction as formation_enthalpy, extended to G. Requires each element’s s298_std_state (the standard-state phase’s molar entropy, per atom – see ThermoDB.s298_std_state) in addition to the usual 0 K atom energies, heats of formation and standard-state H(298 K)-H(0 K); raises MissingReferenceData for any element missing any of them.

Derivation: on the computed energy scale the standard-state element’s Gibbs energy per atom is:

G_std(X, T) = E_ref(X) - DfH0(X, 0K) + [H_std(X, T) - H_std(X, 0)]
              - T * S_std(X, T)

seamm_thermochemistry.fragment_store module#

The fragment-energy store: energies of CBH fragments (and targets) computed earlier, so that a species is computed once per level of theory.

The store is one SQLite file, fragments.db next to the ATcT cache (Parameters/thermochemistry/), and must live on a local disk: SQLite locking is unreliable on GPFS/NFS, so the connection uses no memory mapping, a busy timeout and the rollback journal, and nothing on a compute node ever writes to it. Jobs store their species’ energies as properties on their own configurations (the systems named CBH:<SMILES>); afterwards the single writer, seamm-thermochemistry-fragments harvest <job> on the host, promotes them with insert-or-ignore and full provenance.

A record is keyed by the species identity (canonical SMILES, charge and spin multiplicity) and two opaque fingerprints: the one of the calculation that gave the energy and the one of the calculation that gave the frequencies (ZPE and thermal enthalpy), which can be at different levels. The QC step owns the fingerprint (a hash of the generated input without the lines that do not affect the energy, plus program and version); this module never interprets it. The model chemistry string is kept for people to read. A record without a fingerprint is keyed on the model chemistry and code version instead and flagged unverified: it is used only when nothing verified exists, and the caller is told.

E, ZPE and H(T) are separate fields in kJ/mol. A record whose key already exists is ignored (first value wins); if its values differ from the stored ones by more than 0.1 kJ/mol the attempt is logged in duplicate_log, never overwritten, which catches a different conformer or SCF solution. A value known to be wrong (a wrong conformer, say) is replaced only explicitly, with supersede: the old values move to the superseded table with the date and the reason, and the record keeps its key and id, so nothing is lost and the replacement is visible.

Reads are best effort: a missing, locked or corrupt store gives None and a log message, and the caller computes the species.

seamm_thermochemistry.frequency_corrections module#

Corrections of harmonic frequencies, one frequency at a time, with their sources.

Some methods get their frequencies wrong in a way a single scale factor cannot fix. PM7, for one, puts the X-H stretches near a quarter of their real spread, so its frequencies above 2500 cm^-1 need a steep linear map (Rozanska et al. 2014, eq 1). Such a correction is a piecewise-linear function of the frequency, fitted for a method (and basis), and is applied to each real frequency before the zero-point energy and the thermal functions (H(T) - H(0), S, Cp) are computed.

A correction replaces the ZPE scale factor for its method: the two are never applied together. Each entry carries its citation, the elements its fit set contained, and the frequency window where the map is discontinuous or was not validated, so the thermochemistry step can warn about frequencies there. Its tag (id, version and the range used) goes into the frequency fingerprint of the corrected thermal data, so corrected and uncorrected data are never mixed.

The functions are looked up like the ZPE scale factors, by the (method, basis) of the frequency level’s model chemistry (seamm_thermochemistry.zpe.split_level()).

seamm_thermochemistry.geometry_store module#

Geometries and energies in the fragment store: a species’ energy at one level of theory on a geometry from another.

The fragment table holds E, ZPE and H of a species at one level of theory, or at an energy level and a frequency level, with no geometry. Thermochemistry often wants the energy at a costlier level (e.g. revDSD-PBEP86 or CCSD(T)-F12) on the geometry and frequencies of a cheaper one (e.g. r2SCAN-3c), and the same geometry serves several energy levels. Two tables hold that:

geometry

One row per conformer of a species at a geometry/frequency level. The key is (species, charge, multiplicity, frequency fingerprint, conformer label). The row holds the coordinates and their hash (seamm_util.fingerprint.geometry_fingerprint()), the energy, the unscaled ZPE and H(T) at that level, and when known the harmonic frequencies, the number of imaginary modes, the rotational symmetry number and the moments of inertia, so that H(T) at other temperatures (and later S and G) can be recomputed. ZPEs are stored unscaled; factors are applied at use.

energy

One row per single-point energy on a geometry, keyed by the geometry’s hash and the calculation’s settings fingerprint. The hash, not the row id, is the key, so copies of the store on other hosts keep working; the row id of the geometry is kept as a convenience. Other results of the same calculation (e.g. the MP2 energy of a CCSD(T) run, or the (T) correction) go in byproducts.

A composite energy (a recipe of several components) is never stored, only its components; it is combined at use, as ZPE factors are applied at use, so a corrected recipe or factor needs no recomputation.

Guards, applied when rows are written:

  • An energy whose calculation recorded the geometry it ran on (its geometry fingerprint) must be on the geometry of the row it is attached to; otherwise it is refused.

  • A geometry row is optimized and has its frequencies at one level: when the optimization’s model chemistry is given it must be the frequency level’s. (The settings fingerprints of the two may differ legitimately, e.g. a Hessian by finite differences converges the SCF more tightly, so the model chemistry and program are compared, and both fingerprints are kept.)

  • A geometry with imaginary frequencies is stored, so that its provenance is kept, but geometries() does not return it for reuse unless asked: modes up to IMAGINARY_TOLERANCE (cm⁻¹) are taken as numerical noise.

Writes follow the fragment table’s rules: insert-or-ignore, a differing duplicate (by more than 0.1 kJ/mol) logged in row_history and never overwritten, and an explicit supersede with a reason that keeps the old values in row_history.

Model chemistries are stored as the bare model and the program, e.g. CMP@R2SCAN-3C and ORCA, however they are given (split_model_chemistry()): the ORCA sub-steps name a level CMP@R2SCAN-3C and the Model Chemistry step ORCA:CMP@R2SCAN-3C. Lookups by model chemistry accept either spelling and also match rows written before the names were normalized.

The fingerprint arguments of the readers take one fingerprint or several (any iterable): a code may give a species one of a few fingerprints at the same level (e.g. ORCA with or without NoCOSX, chosen by the species’ elements), and the caller passes those it expects.

Import ORCA atom-energy results from a per-job results CSV.

Expects the shape produced by the atom-energy scan flowchart: one row per element (Atomic Number, Element, Multiplicity), and one "E DFT@<method>/<basis> (kJ/mol)" / "S^2 DFT@<method>/<basis>" column pair per method+basis combination that has been run so far (blank cells are jobs not finished/reached yet, not errors). method and basis are stored separately – method as the ThermoDB method, basis as settings – since ORCA (unlike Gaussian’s composite-method columns) always keeps them as independent axes.

Vetting, since a bad SCF root (wrong occupation, not just unconverged) is the actual failure mode seen for hard atoms (lanthanides etc.), not just a missing value:

  • A computed S^2 is compared to the multiplicity’s expected S(S+1). Within warn_threshold (relative) it’s added silently; beyond that but within reject_threshold it’s added but flagged in “spin_warnings” for manual review; beyond reject_threshold it’s not added at all (“rejected”).

  • A missing S^2 is fine for a singlet (trivially 0, commonly not reported) but rejected for any open-shell multiplicity – no way to vet it.

  • If the CSV’s own multiplicity for an element disagrees with what’s already in the element table (when known), that’s noted in “multiplicity_mismatches” but does not block the import – it may just mean the reference table’s own value needs a look.

Duplicate handling, since re-running elements/methods is expected across “hundreds of these”: an existing (element, code, method, ref_type, settings) value that’s already in the database is left alone if the new value is close (“unchanged”); if it differs by more than energy_rel_tol/energy_abs_tol it is a conflict, resolved one of three ways (force and prefer_lower_energy are mutually exclusive – pick one):

  • Neither set (default): reported in “conflicts”, not overwritten. Safest, but needs a human to look at every conflict.

  • force=True: always overwritten with the new value, listed in “updated”. Only right when you’re confident the new run is simply better across the board – a blanket “latest wins” that can silently make things worse (see prefer_lower_energy).

  • prefer_lower_energy=True: for hard atoms (partially-filled d/f shells – lanthanides, some transition metals) two independently converged runs of the same nominal state can land on genuinely different self-consistent solutions, invisible to the S^2 gate since both have the right total spin. The tell is a conflict whose delta is nearly constant across several basis sets for the same element – not a basis-incompleteness effect, a different orbital occupation. There’s no rigorous variational principle for DFT, but “prefer whichever energy is lower” is the standard practical criterion for picking between competing self-consistent solutions of the same state. If the new value is lower it’s written and listed in “updated”; if the existing value is already lower it is kept and listed in “kept_existing” – so a blanket rerun can’t silently make a good stored value worse. seamm_thermochemistry.ladder module#

Laddered reference values for CBH reactions (CBH-ANL, Elliott et al. JPCA 127, 1512 (2023), eqs. 8-11).

Only the CBH-0 species (H2, CH4, H2O, NH3, HF, …, and the radical CBH-0 species such as CH3 and OH) take their values from ATcT. Every other reference species gets its value from its own CBH reaction one rung below its size, computed at a high level of theory, anchored to the laddered values of that reaction’s species:

L(M) = H(M) - Σ_i c_i H(i) + Σ_i c_i L(i)

where H are the high-level enthalpies (E + scaled ZPE at 0 K, or with the thermal correction at 298.15 K), c_i the coefficients of M’s CBH-(r-1) reaction (r = M’s own rung, the lowest rung at which M is its own fragment) and L(i) the laddered values of the reaction’s other species, recursively down to the ATcT anchors. Because every laddered value is a linear combination of ATcT anchors plus high-level reaction enthalpies, each one carries its coefficients on the ATcT species, so a reaction’s ATcT uncertainty is cᵀΣc with the ATcT covariance exactly, and a method term for the high-level reaction enthalpies is measured by comparing the laddered values with ATcT for every ladder species that ATcT has.

A ladder is built from the fragment-energy store (the high-level energies and the frequency-level ZPE and thermal corrections, harvested from finished jobs) and saved as a named reference set, a JSON file in ladders/ next to the store, with the provenance of every value.

seamm_thermochemistry.layered_store module#

The fragment store in two layers: the user’s local store and the official one, looked up local first.

fragments.db (the local layer) is the store write-back, harvest, merge and supersede write to. fragments-official.db (the official layer) is published on Zenodo, installed and replaced whole by the installer, and only ever read, without locking (it may sit on a network filesystem). See the P5 design note, docs/developer_guide/campaigns/2026-11-10, §4.

The rules, for rows of the same key in both layers:

R1

The local row is used, unless R2 applies.

R2

The official row has been superseded and the local one equals a value it superseded (within 0.1 kJ/mol): the official row is used, and noted.

R3

Otherwise, values differing by more than 0.1 kJ/mol are noted as a conflict, and the local row is used.

R4

Conformers come from both layers; the lowest E + ZPE wins, a tie within 0.1 kJ/mol going to the local layer (LayeredStore.lowest()). Verified rows in either layer come before unverified ones in either.

R5

Energies pair with geometries only on the same geometry hash: looked up in the geometry’s own layer first, then the other. Every row returned carries its layer, so a caller can say when a geometry and its energy come from different layers.

Notes from R2 and R3 accumulate in LayeredStore.notes for the caller to report.

seamm_thermochemistry.references module#

The supplementary reference library: enthalpies of formation that ATcT lacks, from evaluations, measurements, literature and computed values.

references-official.db is published on Zenodo, installed by the installer and only ever read. references.db is the local layer: user references and computed references not yet published. Both sit beside the ATcT cache in <...>/Parameters/thermochemistry/. The importers that build a library are maintainer tools; users only install the published file.

The file keeps every value a source gives (measurement), the one chosen per species and source (reference), and how derived values were derived (derivation), so every choice can be audited and redone. Uncertainties are stored as the source states them, with their level; the expanded (95 %) uncertainty is computed from them by expanded_uncertainty(), so the policy can change without re-importing anything.

Rules built in (the reference-data pilot, 2026-11-12):

  • Burcat’s database is never a source: a source named Burcat, or a value citing it, is refused.

  • No P species from the online JANAF tables (their PH3 and PH2 sit ~17.5 kJ/mol off the printed edition); P species come from the printed edition.

  • Estimates are stored but never chosen unless accepted, which is recorded with who and when.

  • A later source replaces an earlier one only when its uncertainty is below a third of the earlier one’s and they agree within the earlier one’s (later_source_wins()); both values are kept.

  • Every derived value has its derivation, and ReferenceLibrary.check() recomputes it from the current values of its partners.

  • Ions are stored in ATcT’s stationary-electron convention, converted on entry from the thermal-electron one.

seamm_thermochemistry.resolver module#

Resolving the reference species of CBH reactions: ATcT first, then the supplementary reference library.

The order (the reference-library design, §2, and the 2026-11-12 decisions):

  1. ATcT, the version installed. If every reference is in ATcT, nothing else is used and the result is “ATcT-only”.

  2. The user’s own references (the local layer’s user source), when the job enables them.

  3. The library’s sources by rank: printed JANAF, derived values, CODATA, the NBS tables of 1982, D0, W4-17 (0 K only, used only without a 298.15 K value), the WebBook measurements.

  4. Computed references, when enabled.

One source per element. For each element that ATcT does not have (its gas-phase atom is not in ATcT), the references containing it are taken from the first source, in rank order, that has all of them, so that the source’s shared error cancels in the reaction. A later source still replaces that source’s value for a species where the replacement rule says so (science, 2026-11-12): the element then has several sources, the result is labelled “mixed: several sources” with the sources named, and a one-line note says so. It never blocks. When no single source has them all, each reference takes its own best value, with the same label.

The values come back in the layout of an ATcT entry (dfH0_0K, dfH0_298K, uncertainty_298K at 95 %, id), with atct_coefficients empty (they have no covariance with ATcT) and the library’s details (source, layer, citation, …).

seamm_thermochemistry.zpe module#

Scale factors for harmonic zero-point energies, with their sources.

A harmonic ZPE is too large; scaled by a factor fitted for the level of theory it comes close to the anharmonic one. The factor belongs to the geometry and frequency level (the method and basis of the Hessian), so it is looked up from that level’s model chemistry, e.g. ORCA:CMP@R2SCAN-3C or ORCA:DFT@PBE0-D4/def2-TZVP. Each entry carries its source, and a flag when it is used outside what it was fitted for (another basis, or larger molecules than the fit set); the thermochemistry step prints both and stores them with its results.

The store keeps ZPEs unscaled; the factor is applied when the values are used, so a corrected factor needs no recomputation.

seamm_thermochemistry.formation_enthalpy(composition, H_system, temperature, db, code, method, *, ref_type='atom', settings='', units='kJ/mol')[source]#

DfH(T): enthalpy of formation from the elements, at temperature T.

Generalizes formation_energy(..., anchor=True, anchor_at_0K=True) from the electronic-only, 0 K quantity to a real temperature. The Hess cycle is elements (standard states, T) -> gas atoms (T) -> molecule (T). The gas atoms’ own H(T)-H(0) appears in both halves of the cycle and cancels exactly, leaving the standard-state elements’ thermal enthalpy (the form used by G4 and similar composite methods):

DfH(T) = sum(n_X * [DfH0(X, 0 K) - (H_std(X, T) - H_std(X, 0))])
         - [sum(n_X * E_ref(X)) - H_system]

where H_std is the enthalpy of the element’s standard state, per atom (ThermoDB.element.h298_minus_h0_std_state, halved for the diatomic gases). Equivalently, DfH(T) = sum(n_X * DfH0(X, T)) - [atomization enthalpy at T]; at 298.15 K the two forms use the same tabulated data and agree to rounding.

Approximation: the standard-state H(T)-H(0) is tabulated at 298.15 K only and is scaled linearly in T elsewhere – see _std_state_thermal_enthalpy for the size of the error. At 298.15 K the result is exact.

History: until 2026.10.28 this function subtracted (5/2)RT per atom, the gas atom’s own thermal enthalpy, instead of the standard-state term. That made DfH(298 K) too negative by 1.96 kJ/mol per H, 5.15 per C and 1.86 per O (5.8 kJ/mol for water).

  • method – As in atomization_energy.

  • ref_type – As in atomization_energy.

seamm_thermochemistry.formation_gibbs_energy(composition, G_system, temperature, db, code, method, *, ref_type='atom', settings='', units='kJ/mol')[source]#

DfG(T): Gibbs energy of formation from the elements, at T.

Same Hess-cycle construction as formation_enthalpy, extended to G. Requires each element’s s298_std_state (the standard-state phase’s molar entropy, per atom – see ThermoDB.s298_std_state) in addition to the usual 0 K atom energies, heats of formation and standard-state H(298 K)-H(0 K); raises MissingReferenceData for any element missing any of them.

Derivation: on the computed energy scale the standard-state element’s Gibbs energy per atom is:

G_std(X, T) = E_ref(X) - DfH0(X, 0K) + [H_std(X, T) - H_std(X, 0)]
              - T * S_std(X, T)

(the gas atom at 0 K sits DfH0(X, 0K) above the standard state, and has no ZPE), so:

DfG(T) = G_system - sum(n_X * G_std(X, T))
       = sum(n_X * [DfH0(X, 0K) - (H_std(X, T) - H_std(X, 0))])
         + T * sum(n_X * S298_std_state(X)) / 1000
         - [sum(n_X * E_ref(X)) - G_system]

No atomic entropy is needed: the gas atom is only an intermediate.

Approximations: S298_std_state is used at its tabulated 298.15 K value regardless of T, and the standard-state H(T)-H(0) is scaled linearly from its 298.15 K value (see _std_state_thermal_enthalpy). At T=0 this reduces exactly to formation_energy(..., anchor=True, anchor_at_0K=True) evaluated with G_system in place of the electronic energy.

History: until 2026.10.28 this function subtracted (5/2)RT per atom instead of the standard-state thermal enthalpy; see formation_enthalpy.
