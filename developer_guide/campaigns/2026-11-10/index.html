2026-11-10 – Fragment store P5: write-back, in-job reuse, two layers, ATcT download#

Status: design note, written 2026-11-10 (design). Nothing coded. Paul’s decisions 1-5 below are settled. Science reviewed the note the same evening, and its points are folded in:

  • the per-species expected fingerprint (§2a);

  • the structure guard and distinct conformers (§3);

  • R5 (§4);

  • the uncertainty-method column (§5);

  • the official sanity pass (§6);

  • the validation additions.

Decided 2026-11-10 (Paul): D1-D8 accepted as recommended (see D-items for Paul). Implementation can start with P5a.

Numbering: these phases are the fragment store’s P5 (P5a-P5e). The 11-08 presets plan also had a P5 (long-bond and %[(T)] flags) and a P6 (pilot). Those keep their names and have nothing to do with this note.

Why#

The store (P1-P4, seamm-thermochemistry 2026.11.8.x) is read by the step but filled only by hand with seamm-thermochemistry-fragments harvest. ChemAI job 5684 (the 1694 set, the default preset, a serial Loop, parallel: 'no') shows what that costs:

  • By 18:53 on 11-10, 188 iterations had run 675 species calculations, but only ~170 distinct species. That is about 500 repeats (~75 %): H₂ 114 times, CH₄ 74, HF 58, C₂H₆ 49, CH₃F 39, HCl 35, …

  • Every repeat already existed in the job’s own database as CBH:<SMILES> with its fingerprints. The step never looks there: _compute_species finds the system, then optimizes it and computes its Hessian again.

  • When Science harvested 5684 into the ChemAI store by hand (18:35), iterations 178 and later got CH₄, H₂, C₂H₆ and C₃H₈ from the store (“store; energy store”, same E). Harvesting during a job does give reuse within the job, so write-back would have done the same automatically.

  • The 598 seed rows were not reused, for two reasons:

    • A real grid difference. The sub-step rows were run with ! R2SCAN-3C TIGHTSCF (DEFGRID2). The engine adds TIGHTSCF DEFGRID3 (orca_step/data/orca_mdi.py ACCURACY_KEYWORDS). The fingerprint guard is correct. Paul’s decision on the grid is “(a) now, then (b)”: the DEFGRID3 recompute is under way.

    • Different names for one model chemistry. The sub-step rows say CMP@R2SCAN-3C, but the step asks for ORCA:CMP@R2SCAN-3C. This matters only for the unverified fallback, but it will bite every name-based lookup.

  • A third miss, by elements. The step looks up every species’ energy-level energy with the target’s settings fingerprint. _energy_level_energies passes the target’s record fingerprint to store.energy. But ORCA chooses exact exchange (NoCOSX) per calculation by its own elements: a one-center job, or any atom of Na, Mg, Zn, B or P (orca_step#44). So the stored revDSD energies of boron species have fingerprint sha256:7ca1cbbc…, and those of ordinary species have sha256:1e16835c….

    • CBH fragments are sub-structures of the target, so the miss is for the fragments without those elements under a target with them. A B or P target looks up its CH₄, H₂ and HF with 7ca1… and misses. They are then computed with COSX and stored as 1e16…, so they miss again in every such job.

    • P matters most: PF₆⁻, phosphates and the P-F references.

    • The geometry key is not split: r2SCAN-3c has no exact exchange, so COSX is moot and is dropped from its fingerprint (sha256:6b46…). A hybrid geometry level would split the same way.

The repeats are mostly small molecules, so the saving in core-hours is less than 75 %. Validation run V1 will measure it.

Paul’s decisions (settled)#

  1. Write-back. The step adds each species it computes to the store: the geometry and frequency row, and an energy row per level. The existing guards apply: imaginary modes above 20i are rejected, the pairing and same-level guards hold, and duplicates agree within 0.1 kJ/mol. Jobs on remote clusters are harvested when they come back, by a JobServer stage-out hook or the CLI. A merge tool combines copies of the store on different installations.

  2. In-job reuse. “design should check that a job’s fragments are reused”. Each iteration checks the store as it runs, so write-back gives reuse within a job. Duplicates computed by parallel iterations are acceptable. An optional “in progress” marker per species and fingerprint would let a parallel iteration wait instead.

  3. Two layers. “keep two databases, our ‘official’ one plus the user’s local one, and preferentially get from the user’s”.

    • The official layer is on Zenodo: read-only and versioned. Its sha256 is recorded in the installation, and the installer replaces it whole, as it does thermochemistry.db.

    • The local layer is writable, filled only by write-back and harvest, and never overwritten.

    • Lookup goes to local first, then official.

    Science’s refinements:

    • An official “superseded” mark beats an identical old local row (logged).

    • The same species and fingerprint differing by more than 0.1 kJ/mol is logged as a duplicate.

    • The lowest conformer is chosen across both layers, but energies always come from the same layer as their geometry.

    A CLI exports the local rows that are not in official, with their provenance, for the next official version. The single fragments.db now on each machine becomes its local layer.

  4. ATcT. “ATcT is open access, so we should automatically download what is needed so the user doesn’t have to do anything.”

    • The step or the installer downloads the ATcT version when it is missing. The never-modified-copy rule holds, and the metadata and SHA-256 are kept.

    • The covariance default becomes “fetch if needed”, falling back to quadrature when offline.

    • One installer run sets up ATcT, thermochemistry.db and the seed store.

  5. Model-chemistry names. Lookup, write-back and harvest all use the bare model plus the program column (CMP@R2SCAN-3C + ORCA).

Design#

1. Model-chemistry names (P5a)#

seamm_thermochemistry.fragment_store.split_model_chemistry(text, program=None) returns (model, program):

  • The text splits at its first : only when that colon comes before the first @ and the prefix has no @. So ORCA:CMP@R2SCAN-3C gives (CMP@R2SCAN-3C, ORCA), and ORCA:DFT@B3LYP/bse:def2-SVP gives (DFT@B3LYP/bse:def2-SVP, ORCA).

  • QC@CCSD(T)-F12D_RI/cc-pVTZ-F12 is left whole.

  • Programs are compared case-insensitively and stored as the calculation record writes them (ORCA).

  • An explicit program that contradicts the prefix is logged, and the explicit one is kept.

It is applied everywhere a model chemistry enters or queries the store:

  • FragmentRecord.make, GeometryRecord.make and EnergyRecord.make store the bare model and fill program.

  • In harvest_job, the property name’s model field is split. The program comes from the calculation record, or else from the prefix. The property’s second field is a step name (Thermochemistry), never a program.

  • get, geometries, energy and species_at normalize the model they are given and match model_chemistry = ? AND (program IS NULL OR program = ?). Verified lookups by fingerprint are unaffected.

  • Unverified keys become unverified:<program or ?>:<model>@<version or ?>, one spelling for one level.

2. In-job reuse (P5a, with P5b)#

Before computing a species, the step tries three sources in order: the job’s database, the store (local, then official, see §4), and finally a computation.

The job’s database (new, in cbh_approach: _from_job(system_db, can)):

  • It looks at every system named CBH:<can>; a snapshot merge can leave two. For each one it checks the current configuration first, then the others.

  • Geometry level. It takes the properties total energy, ZPE and H(298.15 K) at #Thermochemistry#<model>, but only when the stored frequency fingerprint (split levels) or settings fingerprint (one level) equals the fingerprint of this run. The configuration must also have no imaginary mode above 20i.

  • Energy level. It takes total energy#Thermochemistry#<level> when the settings fingerprint#Thermochemistry#<level> is one of this species’ expected fingerprints (§2a), not the target’s.

  • It reuses the existing configuration. No new “from the fragment store” configuration is created, as _configuration_from_geometry does today.

  • A fingerprint must match, so results from another code version or other settings are never reused silently. The subflowchart route stores no fingerprints, so it gets no in-job reuse from the job’s database; write-back covers it.

  • The fingerprints are known only after the step’s first engine calculation, which is always the target’s. This is the same constraint the store lookup has now.

This alone fixes 5684-type serial loops, and it needs no store at all.

2a. Per-species expected fingerprints (P5a; Science 11-10)#

Each species is looked up, in the job’s database and in the store, with the fingerprints that ORCA would produce for that species: NoCOSX or not, chosen by its own elements. This applies to the energy level and to the geometry (frequency) level. Only the elements change the settings this way, because geometry, charge and multiplicity are removed before hashing (orca_step.fingerprint).

Interface. The code plug-in predicts; the step never re-implements ORCA’s rules.

  • orca_step: batch.expected_fingerprints(model_chemistry, symbols, *, kind="energy" | "hessian", version, options=None).

    • It builds the ! line and blocks with the same helpers that get_task and the MDI engine use (engine_helpers, exact_exchange_method), and hashes them with orca_calculation_record.

    • It returns a small set: the fingerprint as written, and the one with COSX and NoCOSX dropped. Whether COSX is moot is known only from ORCA’s output (cosx_is_moot), so the stored fingerprint is always one of the two.

    • version comes from the target’s calculation record in the same run (one ORCA per run).

  • seamm_exec: Evaluator.expected_fingerprints(configuration, kind) calls the code’s function when it has one. For codes without it, it returns None, and the step falls back to today’s rule (the target’s fingerprint).

  • thermochemistry_step: _energy_level_energies and _from_job (and the store lookups) pass each species’ set. The store’s energy and geometries accept a set of fingerprints.

Self-check. After computing a species, the step compares the recorded fingerprint with the predicted set. A miss means the prediction has drifted from ORCA’s rules, for example after an orca_step change. The step prints a warning naming the species and both fingerprints. The species is still used and written back under its recorded fingerprint, so a drift costs reuse, never correctness.

The store, per iteration (exists). With write-back (§3), each species is in the local layer seconds after any iteration computes it. That covers what the job’s database cannot:

  • iterations of a parallel Loop, which each get a snapshot seamm.db holding only their own structures;

  • later jobs.

Parallel duplicates. The first wave of a parallel Loop (the iterations running at once) computes the shared fragments concurrently, and later iterations read them. The waste is bounded by iterations-at-once × shared fragments. A claim (“in progress”) table could remove it; see D2.

3. Write-back (P5b)#

One code path. The per-configuration body of harvest_job becomes harvest_configuration(store, configuration, species, *, charge, multiplicity, conformer, job, ...). harvest_job loops over systems and calls it. The step calls it right after each species it computes: the target, every fragment, and the “species only” mode (46e2c3f). Write-back and a later harvest of the same job therefore give the same rows, and the later harvest finds only duplicates. Species that came from the store or from the job’s database are not written again.

Guards:

  • The pairing guard and the same-level guard are as now.

  • Writes are insert-or-ignore, and differences over 0.1 kJ/mol are logged, never overwritten.

  • Write-back refuses a geometry with an imaginary mode above 20i, together with the energy rows on it, and says so in the step output. Paul: “rejected”.

  • The explicit harvest CLI keeps today’s behaviour: such a geometry is stored for provenance and never returned for reuse.

  • Structure guard (Science 11-10). Before a computed species is written, its bonds are perceived again from the optimized coordinates, not taken from the configuration’s bonds, which come from the SMILES.

    • The canonical SMILES without stereo, the charge and the multiplicity must equal the key, also compared without stereo.

    • Unusual centres (hypervalent, metal, high coordination) must have a shortest ligand-ligand distance above the molsystem threshold for atoms too close, as for PF₆⁻.

    • Otherwise the species is refused and the step says why. A fragment that rearranged during its optimization would otherwise become an auto: “lower conformer” (D5) and win every later lookup. Hypervalent and zwitterionic fragments are the likely cases; the 1694 set has 16 hypervalent hydrides.

    • The job keeps using what it computed; only the store refuses it.

    • Perception that fails (no connectivity found) also refuses.

    • The same check runs in harvest_configuration, so the CLI applies it too. --no-structure-check overrides it, with the reason recorded in source.

When the step writes:

  • The parameter fragment store gets three choices:

    • “use and add what is computed” (the new default);

    • “use only” (today’s “use if available”);

    • “ignore”.

  • The local layer is opened writable only if it is on a local filesystem of the machine running the step. seamm_thermochemistry.fragment_store. is_local_filesystem(path) reads the mount table: /proc/mounts on Linux, getmntinfo through os.statvfs/stat -f on macOS.

    • zfs, apfs, ext4, xfs and btrfs count as local.

    • nfs*, gpfs, lustre, beegfs, ceph, cifs/smbfs and fuse.sshfs, and anything unknown, count as not local.

    • [thermochemistry] fragment-store-writable = auto | yes | no in seamm.ini overrides the check.

  • This keeps the reason for the 10-28 rule (SQLite locking is unreliable on GPFS/NFS; the 5398 SIGBUS crashes) and drops its letter (“a single writer; never from a job”). See D1.

  • Several iterations on one host may write at once. Each write is one short transaction per species, using the rollback journal and the existing 30 s busy timeout.

  • A write that still fails is logged and skipped. It never fails the species, and the harvest picks it up later.

  • When the step does not write, the properties stay on the configurations, as now (they are the harvest’s input). The step prints “not added to the store (<fstype>); harvested when the job returns” and writes the marker <job>/harvest_fragments.json (species, levels, why).

Conformers (D5). The geometry key has conformer = '', so today a lower conformer found later is only logged. The proposal:

  • Write-back stores a computed geometry as a new conformer, auto:<geometry hash[:12]>, when both of these hold:

    • its E + ZPE is lower than every stored conformer’s by more than 0.1 kJ/mol;

    • it is structurally distinct from each of them: a symmetry-aware Kabsch RMSD above 0.05 Å (RDKit GetBestRMS over the graph).

  • Lower alone is not enough: finite-difference ZPE noise on the same minimum can reach about 0.1 kJ/mol (Science).

  • It must also pass the structure guard above.

  • _geometry_from_store already takes the lowest E + ZPE.

  • A repeat of the same minimum, or a higher conformer, goes to duplicate_log / row_history as now. Nothing is overwritten.

Remote jobs: the JobServer hook (D6).

  • A generic hook in seamm_jobserver: after _finalize_job_status (that is, after stage-out, so the files are local), the JobServer runs the entry points in org.molssi.seamm.jobserver.job_finished.

  • They run in a background thread with a time limit, and are called as hook(job_id=, wdir=, state=, logger=).

  • Exceptions are logged and never change the job’s status. Nothing thermochemistry-specific goes into the JobServer.

  • The hook must never block the poll loop: compare the 28 GB stage-in that stalled ChemAI for 3.5 h.

  • seamm-thermochemistry registers harvest, which:

    • acts only when harvest_fragments.json exists, in any terminal state, since finished iterations are valid even if the job failed later;

    • copies seamm.db with SQLite’s backup API first (the job database is WAL), as Science did by hand for 5684;

    • harvests into the host’s local layer, and appends the counts to the job’s job.out.

Jobs run without a JobServer (run_flowchart by hand on ARC) are harvested with the existing CLI on the host that holds the local layer.

4. Two layers (P5c)#

Files, in <root>/Parameters/thermochemistry/:

fragments.db

The local layer. The name is unchanged, so today’s files become local layers where they are. It is the only file write-back, harvest, merge and supersede write to.

fragments-official.db

The official layer. The installer installs it and replaces it whole. It is opened mode=ro&immutable=1 (no locking), so clusters can read it in place on GPFS.

Both files get a meta table with these keys: layer (local | official), version (official: CalVer), created, schema and source (the Zenodo DOI). The official file’s sha256 goes in seamm.ini [thermochemistry] fragment-store-sha256, next to database-path, and installer check verifies it. The schema becomes version 3 (the meta table, plus claim if D2 is (b)). Readers of schema-2 files keep working.

``LayeredStore(local, official)`` (new layered_store.py):

  • It has the reader API the step uses: geometries, energy, species_at and get.

  • Every row it returns carries layer.

  • Writes go to local only.

  • It refuses a file whose meta.layer is wrong for its role.

Lookup rules:

R1

The same key in both layers: the local row is used, except under R2.

R2

The official row for the key has been superseded (its superseded or row_history kind superseded), and the local row equals one of the superseded old values within 0.1 kJ/mol. Then the official current row is used, and the step logs “local <species> is the value official <version> superseded on <date>: <reason>”.

R3

The same key, values differing by more than 0.1 kJ/mol, and not R2: local is used and the conflict is logged in the step output. The new CLI duplicates --layers lists such conflicts across the two files.

R4

Conformers: the candidates come from both layers, after the imaginary filter. The lowest E + ZPE wins, and a tie within 0.1 kJ/mol goes to local. Verified rows in either layer come before unverified rows in either.

R5

Energies pair with geometries only on the same geometry hash, never across hashes. They are looked up in the geometry’s own layer first, then in the other layer. The energy fingerprint must still match (§2a). This is Science’s answer of 11-10, and it lets a user’s revDSD single point on an official r2SCAN-3c geometry be reused. Science attached two conditions:

(i) The returned energy row carries its own layer, and the step output shows any mix, e.g. “geometry official 2026.12.1, energy local”.

(ii) If a later official version supersedes that geometry with new coordinates, its hash changes and the local energy is simply not found. A test covers this.

Export, merge and build (CLI ``seamm-thermochemistry-fragments``):

export --out contrib.db [--label TEXT]

Writes the local rows whose key is not in official, and rows that differ from official by more than 0.1 (flagged), with every provenance column and their row_history. --label is free text the user picks. No host or user names are added automatically.

merge SOURCE.db ... [--store TARGET]

Insert-or-ignore of every fragment, geometry and energy row into the target (by default the local layer), with provenance kept and source noting the origin label. Differing duplicates are logged, and supersede history is copied as history rows. It refuses an official file as the target. It is used for copies on several installations (Mac and ChemAI), for folding a campaign store into a local layer, and for building the next official.

official build --version V --previous OLD.db --from A.db B.db ... --out NEW.db

Starts from the previous official and merges the contributions. A differing duplicate stops the build until the maintainer resolves it with supersede and a reason. Then it writes meta, runs VACUUM and writes a .sha256 sidecar. Paul publishes the file on Zenodo, as he does for thermochemistry.db (a new concept record).

migrate [--store FILE]

Makes a dated backup, adds meta, normalizes model names (§1) and fills in program. It reports any key collision, and running it twice changes nothing.

5. ATcT and the installer (P5d)#

One installer run. seamm-thermochemistry-installer install and update, which seamm-manager runs, set up:

  1. thermochemistry.db (as now);

  2. fragments-official.db (new Zenodo concept id; checksum verified, sha256 recorded);

  3. the newest ATcT version, if none is cached (download_all).

check reports all three. The installer never touches fragments.db.

ATcT download:

  • From atct.anl.gov, with SEAMM’s own User-Agent and no spoofing.

  • metadata.json already keeps the source, date, sha256 and the number of entries. An existing version is never overwritten.

  • check recomputes the sha256 against metadata.json, to catch a modified copy.

  • “latest” stays “the newest cached version”, so results do not change silently. A newer TN version arrives only through an explicit update, and old versions are kept.

At run time:

  • If the requested version, or any version for “latest”, is missing, the step downloads it when the machine has a network. Without one, the error names the installer command.

  • Two iterations may race to download. download_all must then treat “already there” as success: today it raises ATcTError.

Access: In October, /api/v1/all/ answered 403 to non-browser clients. On 2026-11-10, one request to the API root with SEAMM’s User-Agent answered 200; /all/ was not tried. Hence D4.

Covariances:

  • The default becomes “fetch if needed”. When the fetch fails (offline, 403 or 429), the independent uncertainties are combined in quadrature. The step says so as a warning, not an error.

  • The results also record how each uncertainty was combined (Science 11-10), so that a Loop’s table cannot silently mix the two:

    • a column “uncertainty method” in the CBH table and the results, with the values “covariance” or “quadrature (no covariance: <reason>)”;

    • the matching result property, so it reaches the results CSV and the dashboard.

  • covariance.json is written atomically and merged on write: re-read it, take the union of the blocks, then os.replace. Parallel iterations write it.

  • A read-only cache keeps the blocks in memory, as now.

  • atct_all.json is never modified. covariance.json is SEAMM’s own cache and only grows.

6. Official v1 (P5e)#

Official v1 is built with official build from the ChemAI local layer after the DEFGRID3 recompute. Its contents are D3. Paul publishes it on Zenodo, and the installer’s concept id is filled in by a seamm-thermochemistry release.

Sanity pass before publishing (Science 11-10; part of official build, which refuses to write the file while any check fails, unless a row is explicitly excluded with a reason):

  • Imaginary modes: no geometry row with a mode above 20i.

  • Multiplicity: checked against the ATcT species where ATcT has the species. BH and SiCl₂ are singlets, but the 1694 set had them as triplets, so no triplet rows for them may get in.

  • Connectivity: the structure guard of §3, run over every row: bonds perceived again, SMILES without stereo, charge and multiplicity.

  • Unusual centres: the shortest ligand-ligand distance.

  • Ions and doublets from 5684 belong in v1: they are checked, not excluded.

The report (rows checked, refused, why) is published with the file.

D-items for Paul#

D1 – Write-back from a running job.

The step writes the local layer directly when it is on a local disk of the machine running the step, and otherwise leaves the species to the harvest. This relaxes the 10-28 rule “no store writes from jobs; one writer” to “no SQLite writes over network filesystems; several writer processes on one host are fine”. Recommended: yes.

D2 – “In progress” marker.

(a) None. (b) A claim table: an iteration that finds a fresh claim polls for up to a time limit, then computes; stale claims are those whose process is dead or whose heartbeat is too old. Recommended: (a). The job’s database covers serial loops. Parallel duplicates are limited to the first wave, and claims bring stale-lock handling across processes and hosts. Revisit if V2 shows a large waste.

D3 – Official v1 contents.

(a) Only rows the presets can reach: the engine (DEFGRID3) r2SCAN-3c geometries with frequencies, and the revDSD energies on them, from the DEFGRID3 recompute plus 5684. That is about 600 species. (b) Also the DEFGRID2 sub-step rows and the campaign stores (F12, B2PLYP, PBE0, GFN2). Recommended: (a). Rows no preset can reach only add size and confusion. (b) can follow in v2 if wanted.

D4 – ATcT source.

(a) atct.anl.gov only. When it refuses, a clear message and the existing download --from FILE. (b) As (a), plus a SEAMM mirror on Zenodo of the unmodified atct_all.json as a fallback. That needs ANL’s permission, a question for the Ruscic/Bross contact that is already open. Recommended: (a) now, and ask about (b).

D5 – A lower conformer found by write-back.

(a) Added as a new conformer row (auto:<hash>), and the lowest is used. (b) First wins, and the lower one is only logged (today). Recommended: (a), with the structure guard and the 0.05 Å distinctness test of §3. It matches “lowest conformer across layers” within a layer, and nothing is overwritten.

D6 – Harvesting remote jobs.

(a) A generic JobServer job_finished hook plus the CLI. (b) The CLI only. Recommended: (a). Without it, results from ARC queues reach no store unless someone remembers.

D7 – Clusters.

ARC’s Parameters is on GPFS. ARC gets the official layer only, read in place (immutable), and no local layer. ARC results reach ChemAI’s local layer through the hook or a harvest or merge on ChemAI. Recommended: yes.

D8 – Names.

fragments.db is local and fragments-official.db is official. fragment store gets the choices “use and add what is computed” (default), “use only” and “ignore”. fragment store file names the local layer. Recommended: yes.

Paul’s decision (11-10): all eight as recommended. D1 yes, D2 (a), D3 (a), D4 (a) and ask ANL about (b), D5 (a) with the structure guard, D6 (a), D7 yes, D8 yes.

Science’s view (11-10): D2 (a), D3 (a), D4 (a) now and ask ANL about (b), D5 (a) with the structure guard, D6 (a), D7 yes. D1 and D8 are engineering calls, and Science has no objection to them. Paul decides.

Science’s answer on R5#

Energies may pair across layers on the same geometry hash: the geometry’s own layer first, then the other. They never pair across hashes, and the energy fingerprint must match. Science added two conditions, the visible layer mix and a test for a superseded geometry (now in R5 in §4).

Tests#

Unit tests need no network: Zenodo and ATcT are mocked.

P5a

  • split_model_chemistry on ORCA:CMP@R2SCAN-3C, a bare name plus a program, ORCA:DFT@B3LYP/bse:def2-SVP, QC@CCSD(T)-F12D_RI/... and a contradicting program.

  • Prefixed and bare names find the same rows. A harvest of a sub-step job and of an engine job gives the same model and program.

  • _from_job: a CBH:C with matching fingerprints is reused (with _compute_species mocked); a mismatched fingerprint is computed; two CBH:C systems work; the energy level is reused; an imaginary mode above 20i is not reused.

  • expected_fingerprints (orca_step): for CH₄, BF₃, PF₆⁻, Na⁺ and a lone atom, at revDSD and at r2SCAN-3c, the predicted set contains the fingerprint recorded by a real ORCA run. The recorded fingerprints come from fixtures cut from 5602 and 5684 outputs, with no ORCA in the tests. Ordinary species give 1e16…, boron and phosphorus species give 7ca1…, and r2SCAN-3c gives the same fingerprint for all.

  • In a P target, the CH₄, H₂ and HF fragments are looked up with 1e16… and hit the stored rows; the P fragments are looked up with 7ca1….

  • A code without expected_fingerprints falls back to the target’s fingerprint.

  • The self-check warning appears when the prediction is made to miss.

P5b

  • Golden: write-back rows equal harvest_job rows for the same job.

  • An imaginary geometry is refused, with its energies.

  • Structure guard: a fragment whose optimized geometry has different connectivity (a fixture of a rearranged hypervalent hydride) is refused. So are a wrong multiplicity (BH as a triplet against the key’s singlet) and an unusual centre with ligands too close (the old bad PF₆⁻ geometry). The good octahedral PF₆⁻ passes.

  • A lower conformer that is structurally distinct gets auto:. The same minimum with E + ZPE lower by 0.12 kJ/mol and RMSD below 0.05 Å goes to the duplicate log. A higher conformer is logged.

  • is_local_filesystem against monkeypatched mount tables.

  • Eight processes writing 50 species each to one temporary store give every row and no error.

  • A store locked past the timeout is logged, and the species still succeeds.

  • The marker is written only when the step did not write.

  • The JobServer hook is discovered, runs off the poll thread, its exceptions are isolated, and a second harvest finds only duplicates.

P5c

  • R1-R5 with two temporary stores, including R2 (official supersede beats an identical old local row) and R3 (logged).

  • The lowest conformer across layers.

  • R5: a local energy on an official geometry (same hash) is used, and the output names both layers. After a newer official supersedes that geometry with new coordinates, the local energy is not found and the species is computed (Science’s condition (ii)).

  • The official file is read-only (a write raises) and opened immutable.

  • export gives only rows that are not in official.

  • Merge round trip: export, then merge into an empty store, gives the source’s rows.

  • migrate on a schema-2 fixture copied from ChemAI’s layout: backup, normalization, idempotence.

V1

A serial 20-compound slice of the 1694 set, default preset, ChemAI queue.

  • It includes at least one P or B target (e.g. a phosphate or a fluoroborane). Its ordinary fragments (CH₄, H₂, HF) must be hits on rows that the ordinary targets computed, and its P/B fragments must be looked up with the NoCOSX fingerprint.

  • Acceptance: each distinct fragment is computed once, and results equal 5684’s within 0.01 kJ/mol. The fingerprint self-check gives no warning, in particular on the ordinary-fragment rows of the P/B target (Science).

  • It also measures the core-hours saved.

  • About 5 core-h.
